Data, definitions and numerical methods

Data and source models

The model specifications follow Example/example.ipynb: linear trend (code cell 5), exponential trend (7), log regression with time, one COVID indicator and log GDP (9), undamped Holt (13), residual Monte Carlo (15, 20, 23, 25), and the annual multiplicative approximation (27, 29). Cell indices are zero-based.

Demand: forecasting_exercise_solution.xlsx, “MC Simulation”, A18:B57. Passenger counts are divided by 1,000,000 to obtain MPPA. The requested 1986–2025 data window is retained; the original notebook’s CSV includes earlier years and ends in 2024, so fitted values need not equal its saved plots.

Synthetic GDP: Example/ZurichDemand.csv, 1986–2024. Currency and price basis are unspecified. The missing 2025 GDP value and later GDP values are scenarios. The regression uses only paired observations available by the selected forecast origin.

Fitting and forecasts

Trend coefficients use ordinary least squares. Linear forecasts can follow the fitted line directly or start at the last demand observation. Exponential trend uses OLS on log demand and the same direct/anchored alternatives.

Holt uses additive trend, no seasonality, no damping and estimated initial states. Fits for every selectable final year are precomputed with the notebook’s exact statsmodels ExponentialSmoothing(...).fit(optimized=True) call in original passenger units. Sliders change the level smoothing weight α and trend smoothing weight β. Initial states remain fixed at the fitted values; historical states and forecasts are recalculated. Restore fitted values returns to the notebook fit. Any optimiser non-convergence is reported beside the chart.

Regression: ln demand = intercept + time coefficient × time + COVID coefficient × I(2020–2022) + GDP coefficient × ln GDP + residual. A single COVID indicator is used, matching the notebook. If the calibration window precedes COVID, its all-zero indicator is omitted. Future indicators are zero. Sliders adjust the intercept, time, COVID and log GDP coefficients while retaining the same predictor definitions. Changing one coefficient holds the others fixed and updates both fitted values and forecasts. Restore fitted values returns to the OLS estimates. Forecasts exponentiate predicted log demand directly, without a mean-bias adjustment.

GDP scenario A holds the last supplied value constant. Scenario B fits a straight line to log GDP and directly extrapolates it. These scenarios do not imply a causal relationship.

Simulation conventions

Monte Carlo uses 5,000 independent paths, normal residual draws, sample standard deviation (n−1 denominator), and pointwise 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles. Level-space residuals are added to the linear/Holt baseline, then clipped to zero. Log residuals multiply the exponential/regression baseline after exponentiation. Residual shocks do not accumulate.

The notebook’s GBM implementation is an annual Euler–Maruyama approximation: Y(next) = max(0, Y(current) × (1 + μ + σε)). It estimates μ as the mean annual log return and σ as its sample standard deviation. This is not the exact exponential GBM transition. The COVID option excludes returns by ending year only; 2023 remains. Zero is possible and absorbing. The drift-only curve uses repeated multiplication by (1 + μ).

The browser uses a seeded pseudorandom generator, not NumPy’s generator. Equations and distribution conventions match the notebook; individual random paths differ. Fixed seeds make browser results reproducible.

Fitted-parameter uncertainty and future structural breaks are omitted. Bands describe individual forecast years, not simultaneous coverage of an entire path. Holdout RMSE evaluates the displayed forecast curve, or the simulated median, on observed years after the forecast origin. Annual data cannot identify within-year seasonality.
